Impact and Success

We streamlined core operations, reducing job friction by 36%. A new Task & Job Scheduler fixed unassigned tasks, duplicates, and coordination gaps — lowering scheduling errors by 34%. We also built Swivl's first scalable design system and UX operating model, boosting team efficiency by 28%.

The Ask

Swivl initially asked us to create a foundational design system and resolve friction within their existing product. On the surface, this seemed like a UI consolidation exercise — aligning components, visuals, and workflows. However, deeper exploration showed that the challenge extended beyond interface polish.

Reframing the Problem

Swivl engagement revealed systemic UX gaps, shifting focus from design system to scalable operating model, workflows, ownership, and delivery foundations.

UX Enablement: Strategy, Methods, and Why It Mattered

To address Swivl's early-stage challenges, we executed a structured UX Enablement exercise designed to diagnose how design operated inside the organization — not just how the product behaved. This allowed us to uncover systemic inefficiencies and propose a strategy that supported both team maturity and product scalability.

Swivl initially approached us to build a design system, the UX Enablement analysis revealed deeper structural gaps: unclear ownership, inconsistent decision-making, siloed designers, late engineering involvement, and no shared patterns or rituals. These issues limited quality and made delivery unpredictable. By identifying these systemic blockers early, we shifted the engagement toward building foundational workflows, role clarity, and a scalable UX operating model.

a. Stages Of UX Maturity

Framing the Core Challenges

The challenges span three areas — People, Process, and System — revealing gaps in design leadership and skills, siloed workflows, and an unstructured design system that create inefficiencies and inconsistent output. Because each issue differs in impact and complexity, we prioritise them using leverage — a measure of how strongly solving an issue improves quality and delivery, and how feasible it is to act on.

Proposed Strategy

Swivl's challenges span People, Process, and System gaps — specifically weak design leadership, skill gaps, siloed workflows, and an unstructured design system. By prioritising interventions through leverage, we focus first on People challenges, which cascade into stronger processes and systems. This forms the basis for a phased improvement roadmap.

Immediate Actions (0–3 Months)

  • Introduce a structured design workflow across research → ideation → wireframes → prototypes → testing → handoff
  • Define clear Designer vs PM responsibilities using a RACI matrix
  • Improve design–engineering collaboration through async channels
  • Conduct quick internal or lightweight user testing to identify early usability issues
  • Build a centralized design system to eliminate inconsistencies

Mid-Term Plan (3–6 Months)

  • Hire or assign a Senior Design Lead to set direction and unify design quality
  • Standardize handoffs with detailed rationale, edge cases, and interaction flows
  • Establish iterative feedback loops through structured reviews and retrospectives
  • Align design KPIs with business metrics and run regular cross-team strategy syncs

Long-Term Plan (6–12 Months & Beyond)

  • Scale the team with specialized roles — UX researchers, UI designers, interaction designers
  • Strengthen UX culture through critiques, workshops, and cross-functional collaboration
  • Expand the design system into a fully coded component library for engineering reuse and platform-wide consistency
